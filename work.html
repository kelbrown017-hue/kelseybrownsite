I can work in both in ArcGIS and QGIS for spacial analysis.

  • Surveillance and Inequality in New York City — This is a paper about surveillance cameras in NYC, using data collected by Amnesty International. While cameras appear evenly distributed across the city’s street grid at first glance, running a kernel density estimation and hotspot analysis (using the Getis-Ord Gi* statistic) reveals clear spatial clustering. I overlaid the hotspots onto census tract data, and found that surveillance was disproportionately concentrated in nonwhite, low-income, and high-poverty neighborhoods. For example, 85% of hotspots fell in high-nonwhite tracts, even though those tracts made up only 57% of the city.

Algorithms I use are: k-nearest-neighbor, naive Bayes, decision trees and random forests, logistic regression, multiple regression (including ridge and lasso methods), support vector machines. What I love about ML is the logic of model building and testing, and I am always thinking about how these tools can be used for social and behavioral questions!

  • Comparing Classifiers on Online Shopper Purchase Intent — In this paper, my goal was to predict whether a person would complete a purchase based on their browsing behavior during a single e-commerce session (using the Online Shoppers Purchasing Intention Dataset from UCI). My secondary goal was exploratory, and it was to build a bunch of ensembles using different algorithms to get a sense of the best algorithm for the dataset. I built three bagged homogenous ensembles using k-nearest-neighbor, a boosted decision tree, and logistic regression, then combined them into a weighted heterogeneous ensemble. The boosted decision tree was the strongest model (AUC = 0.930, F1 = 0.669), and the heterogeneous ensemble narrowly edged it out on AUC (0.932) with an almost identical F1 (0.668).
